01The first move

Why nobody froze the stablecoins

Tether and Circle can freeze USDT and USDC, but only while the attacker holds them. On Ethereum, the attacker held them for about 21 minutes. The swaps ran through 0x7c96…3E1C, a wallet upgraded with EIP-7702 (MetaMask's StatelessDeleGator) that batched approvals and swaps.

02The pipeline

Round lots, then a spray

The ETH was split into round-number storage wallets (10,000 / 10,000 / 9,000…) and left alone for 2.5 days. On 27 Sep at 07:40 UTC, Exploiter 5 started draining into fresh wallets in 11–405 ETH pieces. Exploiter 4 followed through the same intermediate wallets (0xf7bC…96C3, 0x4a77…d330): one operator, one pipeline. We traced 246 fresh wallets, none of them labeled on Etherscan at the time.

Gross flows into each exit from attacker wallets, 27–28 Sep, USD at ETH $2,697. Skip:Go's contracts (CCTPRelayer, SkipGoSwapRouter, GoFastHandler) were identified from verified source code.

03The money shot

The first 15 Bitcoin addresses

Every THORChain deposit carries a memo that says where the swapped coins go. Decoding the attacker's 288 ETH-side deposits showed 7,761 ETH (≈ $20.9M) swapped into BTC and sent here. Part 2 found 111 more on the XRP side, 6 of them shared with this list.

06Method

How, and what we don't claim

Method
  • Starting points: Etherscan-labeled "Bitget Exploiter" wallets.
  • Followed ETH and major tokens (>1,000 holders) from 27 Sep 07:00 UTC.
  • Kept only wallets first seen after the hack; excluded THORChain vaults, busy services and anything with pre-hack history.
  • Filtered out address-poisoning fakes.
  • BTC destinations decoded from THORChain memos.
Not claimed
  • Attribution. That's Bitget's and TRM's; see Part 3 for the shared infrastructure.
  • That any market maker, bridge or service acted knowingly.
  • Final numbers. Funds were moving at publication.
