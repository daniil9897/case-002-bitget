01What happened
Update, 28 Sep: ZachXBT later published his own findings on the launderers (post), and Bitget raised the loss to $387.5M. The figures below are as of Part 1. Part 2 continues the trail →

On 24 September 2026, at 18:31 UTC, money started leaving Bitget's hot wallets. By the time the exchange noticed, $351.6M was gone across seven chains. Bitget's CEO said the attacker compromised a backend system and spoofed transaction data to get withdrawals approved. Bitget and TRM Labs point to North Korea.

The next day, ZachXBT, crypto's best-known on-chain investigator, said publicly he would not monitor this one.

So I followed the Ethereum side myself.

This page covers only the Ethereum leg: roughly 24,600 ETH and $47.6M in stablecoins. Every number below comes from public blockchain data, and every claim links to a transaction you can check yourself.

03The swap

Why nobody froze the stablecoins

Tether and Circle can freeze USDT and USDC, but only while the attacker still holds them. On Ethereum, the attacker held them for about 21 minutes. The swaps went through the EIP-7702-upgraded wallet 0x7c96…3E1C (MetaMask's StatelessDeleGator), which batched approvals and swaps into single calls.

04The pipeline

One operator, one pipeline

Exploiters 4 and 5 route through the same intermediate wallets (e.g. 0xf7bC…96C3, 0x4a77…d330). Tracing five-plus hops out found 246 fresh wallets. None of them was labeled on Etherscan at the time of writing.

06The money shot

15 Bitcoin addresses

Every THORChain deposit carries a memo that says where the swapped funds should go. Decoding all 288 of the attacker's deposits shows 7,761 ETH (≈ $20.9M) swapped into BTC and sent to these 15 addresses. If they touch an exchange, they can be frozen.

08Side note

The hacker is being hacked

Address-poisoning bots found the attacker's wallets within minutes. After every real transfer they send fake tokens with Unicode look-alike names from addresses that share the first and last characters of the real destination, hoping the attacker copies the wrong one from their history.

10Method

How this was done, and what it doesn't claim

Method

  • Starting points: Etherscan-labeled "Bitget Exploiter" wallets.
  • Followed outgoing ETH and major tokens (>1,000 holders) from 27 Sep 07:00 UTC onward.
  • Kept only fresh addresses (first activity after the hack). Stopped at contracts and services.
  • Excluded THORChain vaults, addresses with 400+ transactions, and anything with pre-hack history.
  • Fake tokens from address poisoning filtered out.
  • BTC destinations decoded from THORChain deposit memos.

What this does NOT claim

  • Attribution. Bitget and TRM point to North Korea; I haven't independently verified that.
  • That any market maker, bridge or service acted knowingly.
  • Anything about the XRP (~$158M), Arbitrum, BSC, TRON, Avalanche, Optimism or Base legs. Not traced yet.
  • Final numbers. Funds were moving at publication time.
